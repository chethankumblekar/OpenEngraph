## Where OpenEngraph is ahead

> ⚠️ **Strategic flag — re-read before quoting the uniqueness claim externally.** OpenEngraph is not the *only* OSS, fully-local, hybrid (graph + embeddings) tool: `codebase-memory-mcp` and `LocalNest MCP` already held this status, and this run adds a third OSS local-first hybrid peer, `code-graph-mcp` (sdsrss) — AST graph + BM25 + optional local vector search fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion. The claim still holds cleanly against every *commercial* competitor (Augment, Cody, Cursor, Unblocked all still send the embeddings step to the cloud), so the positioning doc's core argument is not wrong, just narrower than "every sampled competitor" — it should say "every commercial competitor and most OSS peers" rather than "every entrant."

- Against the **commercial** tier: still the only entrant that is simultaneously open-source, fully local (including the embedding step), and hybrid. Every commercial hybrid player (Augment, Cody, Cursor, Unblocked) remains closed-source and cloud-dependent for the embedding half.
- Against the **OSS/local** tier: no longer unique on hybrid retrieval alone (now three OSS peers share some version of it — `codebase-memory-mcp`, `LocalNest MCP`, and `code-graph-mcp`), but still has a graph-first *query router* — structural questions resolve deterministically via the graph before falling back to embeddings — rather than a flat fusion of signals. Whether that's a materially better retrieval architecture than peers' approaches is a product question this research run can't settle; flagging for a human call rather than asserting an advantage the research doesn't support.
- Cheapest possible trust story for security-conscious/regulated users remains true in absolute terms (no subscription, no code leaving the machine), but it's now shared by most OSS peers tracked here, not unique to OpenEngraph.

## Where OpenEngraph is behind (by design — enterprise-roadmap scope)

- No multi-repo/org-wide graph (Augment, Cody, Port/Cortex/Backstage all have this; `codebase-memory-mcp` has a partial/opt-in version too; and this run's new entrant `kivgraph` makes symbol-resolved cross-repo graph traversal its *entire* reason to exist — the first OSS local-first peer for which multi-repo is the core differentiator rather than an add-on).
- No cross-system connectors (Unblocked remains the clear commercial leader: GitHub/GitLab/Bitbucket/Slack/Jira/Confluence/Notion/Google Drive, and now Windsurf as an additional supported MCP client).
- No temporal/commit-lineage intelligence (Augment's "Context Lineage" is the direct commercial analog, now confirmed official — see change log) — **and still also behind two OSS peers on some version of this**: `codebase-memory-mcp`'s git-diff impact mapping and `LocalNest MCP`'s time-travel knowledge graph. Neither is full commit-lineage history, but both narrow the "this is purely enterprise-roadmap, no rush" framing for this specific item.
- No infrastructure intelligence (Cortex/Port/Backstage's whole reason to exist; `codebase-memory-mcp` has a partial K8s/Docker-Compose version).
- Narrower language coverage than most OSS peers — codebase-memory-mcp (162), Graphify (~40), CodeGraph (34), and now also the two new entrants, `code-graph-mcp` (19) and `kivgraph` (5), all exceed OpenEngraph's 3 (TypeScript/Python/Go). This remains the most actionable near-term gap: it's OSS-Phase-1 scope, not enterprise-roadmap scope, and existing plugins already establish the pattern for adding a language.
